# Recaps and music-led montages

Use for event recaps, highlights, promos or music-led sections inside a longer story. Adapt to the audience, brief and available material; do not impose a fixed runtime, frame rate, shot length, category order or loudness.

## Select the moment before setting its duration

Contact sheets locate candidates; review motion around each candidate to identify anticipation, action, outcome and reaction. For a throw, preserve the release and landing/result when that is the payoff; for a spoken sign-off, retain the words and useful gesture or reaction. Shift the source window to the stronger moment before simply lengthening it. A fast edit can hold a complete action for several beats and cut a brief detail much faster.

Build short runs of related activities, with varied angle, scale and movement. Follow requested section order; otherwise infer a simple progression from the event rather than shuffling unrelated shots for variety. Two distinct moments from one recording can make a useful sequence unless repeats are prohibited. Trim idle camera searches and accidental obstructions; check horizon, crop and subject clearance in motion before adding leveling or stabilization. Preserve intentional handheld energy.

## Fit picture to the actual music

Identify audible beats, accents and phrase changes in the chosen cue. Beat detection and tempo estimates are candidate aids, not listening or proof of phase. Account for the music's current source in-point/slip, timeline placement, speed and any internal edits. Recheck the beat map after those change.

For an unchanged-speed music segment, a source beat at time B maps to timeline time `segment_start + B - source_in`. For constant playback speed S (1 at normal speed), divide the source-time difference by S. Map separately across music edits and use the actual timing map for variable speeds. Convert each absolute boundary to a timeline frame independently; repeatedly adding a rounded beat/shot duration accumulates drift. Keep exact source and timeline rates distinct.

Choose a nearby beat that lets the action resolve, or shift the source window so the payoff fits. Use faster subdivisions or longer phrases to vary energy rather than assigning every shot the same beat count. Playback around the cuts must confirm rhythm and continuity; metadata, equal durations and a tempo estimate do not establish a beat-matched edit. If listening/playback is unavailable, label beat alignment provisional.

## Land the ending

Reconcile the latest requested emotional ending with branding. A thank-you can lead into an animated end card, or a card can precede a required final live-action scene; honor explicit order and preserve the user's later manual choice. Do not decide that every recap must end on either a logo or a face. Trim animations to intentional entry/exit points with enough time to read, and use transitions at boundaries when they serve the chosen style rather than adding them to every cut.

Fit the continuous music to the revised sections and ending. Duck or fade around a spoken moment for intelligibility; check the return after speech so it does not create an unexplained dip. Retain complete words and a useful reaction, and let the final music/card resolve deliberately. Coordinate channel mapping and encoded listening with Fairlight when available; the sign-off's presence on a track or nonzero waveform does not prove it is intelligible.

## Review the cut in its own terms

For footage-led review, use elapsed time, shot/action, intended change and status. A useful row is: `Revision B, 00:18–00:22 | ball throw | keep the landing and smile; shift earlier or hold to the next suitable beat | pending`. Use dialogue rows only for spoken sections. Reconcile notes against timeline instances and source ranges, including repeated sources and manual crops.

Review the assembled sequence at speed for action completion, visual grouping, rhythm, framing, transitions and emotional landing. When export is authorized or a current matching render exists, sample the encoded picture and listen to the music joins and spoken ending; otherwise defer encoded checks without rendering. Disclose partial coverage; stills, decode success and loudness measurements cannot substitute for motion and listening review.
